How a blood test detects cancer ENC 2256 · University of Florida

Liquid biopsy

Finding cancer in a tube of blood: what it is, how labs find tumor DNA, and where it is used today.

Tissue biopsy

The traditional standard: a sample cut or drawn from the tumor itself. It is invasive, and some tumors are hard to reach.2

Liquid biopsy

A minimally invasive test of a body fluid, usually blood, that can be repeated to track a cancer over time.1

1 · Definition

What is a liquid biopsy?

A liquid biopsy tests a sample of body fluid for material shed by a cancer, instead of cutting into the tumor.1

Fluids that can be tested

Blood /Urine /Saliva /Spinal fluid /Amniotic fluid /Abdominal fluid

What it looks for1

Circulating tumor cells that broke away from the tumor

Cell-free DNA fragments floating in the fluid

Extracellular vesicles, tiny packages cells release

What it is used for1

Diagnosis, including early detection

Matching patients to the right therapy

Monitoring a cancer over time

2 · Source

Where tumor DNA comes from

a

Tumor cells release DNA into the blood as they grow and die.

b

Circulating tumor DNA carries the same mutations as the tumor.2

c

It mixes with DNA from healthy cells, which everyone has. Cancer patients have more cell-free DNA overall.2

3 · Method

How the lab finds it

  1. 1

    Draw blood

    A routine blood draw; plasma is separated from the cells.

  2. 2

    Read the DNA

    Cell-free DNA is sequenced at targeted methylation regions.

  3. 3

    Classify the pattern

    Machine learning flags a cancer signal and predicts where it came from.3

What is methylation? Cells place small chemical tags on their DNA. Cancer cells tag it in different patterns, so reading the tags can reveal a cancer and the tissue it started in.3

4 · In use

Matching patients to treatment

Blood sample from a patient with advanced cancer

Tumor DNA is profiled for genetic changes

Matched to a targeted drug that fits those changes

In 2020, the FDA approved two blood tests that do this, FoundationOne Liquid CDx and Guardant360 CDx, then expanded their approved uses to more cancers and drugs.4

A negative result is not the last word. If the blood test misses a targetable change, the FDA advises testing the tumor tissue.4

5 · In use

Screening for colorectal cancer

In July 2024, the FDA approved Shield as the first blood test for primary colorectal cancer screening, for average-risk adults aged 45 and older.6

Results in 7,861 adults compared with colonoscopy5

Specificity for advanced neoplasia: people without it correctly cleared89.6%
Sensitivity for stage I to III colorectal cancer87.5%

The blood test catches most cancers but few precancerous growths, and any positive result must be followed by a colonoscopy.5,6

6 · Limits

What it cannot do yet

Tumor DNA is scarce

At around 0.01% of circulating DNA, the tumor's signal is easy to miss, especially when tumors are small.2

Many cancers are missed

The multi-cancer test caught about half of cancers (51.5%), so a negative result does not rule cancer out.3

Tissue may still be needed

When a blood test finds no targetable change, the tumor tissue should be tested.4

It does not replace colonoscopy

Shield found only 13.2% of precancerous lesions, and positive results need a colonoscopy.5,6
